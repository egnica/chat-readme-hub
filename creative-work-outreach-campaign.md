# Creative Work Outreach Campaign

Date: October 9, 2026

## Goal

Find additional paid work that uses my skills and helps me reduce
my reliance on bartending at the Lexington.

Part-time video editing is especially appealing, but I am also open
to marketing support, website work, recurring freelance projects,
new clients, or a more substantial opportunity. I do not need to
choose one job title before starting outreach.

## Context

The Lexington has provided essential supplemental income, and I am
grateful for the people and places that have supported me throughout
my career. However, having Friday, Saturday, and Sunday committed
to that work feels draining. I want more of my working time to
contribute to the creative and technical work I want to pursue.

After being laid off, I previously created an introduction video
and an interactive video sequence telling my story. That effort
did not generate the help or opportunities I hoped for.

This campaign should account for that experience. I now have a
better communication and tracking system, including contact IDs
in email links. Better measurement can help me understand engagement,
but it does not guarantee paid work.

## Campaign Concept

Create a short personal video and a dedicated splash page that
people can visit and share.

The page would connect visitors to relevant portfolio and video
examples and provide an easy way to contact me or book a conversation.

The campaign can invite both:

- Direct opportunities for paid work.
- Introductions to people who might need my help.

Use the same core video and page across outreach channels, adapting
the accompanying message to the audience.

## Video Direction

- Explain the kind of opportunities I am seeking.
- Keep room for employment, freelance work, and client projects.
- Include brief, genuine appreciation for the work that has
  supported me.
- Focus on what I can offer and what I want to move toward.
- Decide how explicitly to mention bartending or the Lexington.
- End with a clear request to connect, introduce me, or discuss work.

The script, length, and final wording are still undecided.

## Outreach Approach Discussed

### Personal connections

Reach out to people who may know potential employers or clients.

Laura King is one possible contact. Discuss asking for her perspective,
introductions, or a share of the video. Paying her to promote a post
was raised as an idea, but no decision was made and no service was
verified.

### Businesses with potential overflow work

Research agencies, production companies, and other relevant businesses.

Possible needs include:

- Video editing.
- Turning podcasts or longer videos into clips.
- Website updates.
- Ongoing marketing support.

Tailor the email to the work that could be useful to that business.

### Social and newsletter

Publish the video on social channels, including LinkedIn, with a
link to the page.

The working recommendation is a dedicated outreach campaign plus
a short newsletter mention. Final audience selection and overlap
still need to be worked out.

## Campaign Size

Do not impose an arbitrary ten-person limit.

Once the assets are finished, the existing email system makes
broader distribution practical. Choose the audience based on
relevance, available contacts, and capacity to follow up.

No final recipient count has been selected.

## Evaluating Results

Prioritize replies, introductions, conversations about paid work,
and actual opportunities.

Use page visits and clicks as supporting evidence. Automated email
activity can muddy those signals.

Review which audiences and messages produce useful responses, then
adjust the outreach. The purpose is to create income opportunities
that can replace Lexington shifts; no income target or exit date
was set during this conversation.
